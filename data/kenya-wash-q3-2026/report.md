# Emergency Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Response in Turkana, Marsabit and Garissa

*Northbridge Relief, Kenya country office. Quarterly report for the period July to September 2026. Award number NR-KE-2026-031. Version 1.2, 12 October 2026.*

## 1. Overview

The third quarter of 2026 was the hottest and driest of the year in northern Kenya. Livestock losses continued and many families moved further from their homes in search of water and pasture. Against this background, Northbridge Relief restored water supply at thirteen boreholes, which now serve 19,067 people. Hygiene promotion teams reached about 8,800 people with household and community sessions, and 2,313 hygiene kits were distributed to families with young children.

The response is designed with the county governments and follows the national guidelines for emergency water trucking and borehole rehabilitation. Water trucking was phased out in Turkana during the quarter as rehabilitated boreholes came back into use, which lowered the cost of the response and gave communities a more reliable supply.

The project works in arid and semi-arid counties where most households depend on livestock and where shallow wells dry up quickly in the long dry season. Its approach is to bring broken boreholes back into use, hand them to trained community committees, build household latrines with families and run hygiene promotion so that safer water leads to better health. Schools are included because children spend much of the day there and often have no water on site.

## 2. Progress by result area

### 2.1 Water supply

Rehabilitation work was completed at 13 boreholes this quarter against a target of 16, or 43 percent of the target. In Turkana, six boreholes were rehabilitated, with new solar pumps fitted at three sites. In Turkana alone, the rehabilitated boreholes serve 8.5 thousand people.

Across the three counties, the rehabilitated water points give safe water to 19,067 people: 10,032 women and girls and 9,035 men and boys. Waiting times at the water points fell sharply once the second tap stands were installed, and women's groups in Marsabit reported less conflict at the water points in the evening.

Water management committees took over the running of the rehabilitated boreholes. The project trained 105 committee members in operation, basic repairs and fee collection, 49 women and 46 men, so that every committee now has at least two women in leadership roles.

Water quality testing found that 92 percent of samples met the national standard for drinking water, against a target of 95 percent. Garissa had the lowest result (96%), because two shallow wells near the river were contaminated after the August floods; the team chlorinated them and will test them again in October.

### 2.2 Sanitation

Families built 1,204 household latrines with materials and technical support from the project, up from 1,016 in the previous quarter. This is 89 percent of the quarterly target. Latrine construction was slower in Garissa, where flooding in early August damaged 37 pits that had already been dug. Community-led sanitation sessions were held in 48 villages, and nine villages were declared open-defecation free.

### 2.3 Hygiene promotion

Hygiene promoters ran sessions on handwashing, safe water storage and menstrual hygiene in all three counties. In total, 8,872 people took part this quarter: 3,497 in Turkana, 2,438 in Marsabit and 2,973 in Garissa. Women and girls made up 55 percent of participants. Over 2,400 hygiene kits were handed out at the same sessions, each containing soap, a 20-litre jerrycan and water treatment tablets.

Promoters used picture cards and radio spots in Turkana and Somali, and the sessions were timed to fit around water collection and livestock watering. Mothers' groups asked for more sessions on safe storage of drinking water, which will be added to the curriculum next quarter.

### 2.4 Schools

In total, 8 schools now have functional handwashing stations, against a target of 15. Head teachers in Marsabit reported that attendance improved once water was available on site, although the project does not yet measure this.

## 3. Accountability to affected people

Community members can raise concerns through the toll-free line 0800 721 316, suggestion boxes at water points, or directly with hygiene promoters. The project logged 163 pieces of feedback this quarter, most of them requests for more water points. Complaints about water fees at two boreholes in Marsabit were resolved by renegotiating the fee with the committees.

## 4. Challenges

Insecurity along the Marsabit to Moyale road stopped field visits for eleven days in September. Spare parts for solar pumps had to be brought from Nairobi, which delayed work at two boreholes until the next quarter. Diesel prices rose sharply, and generator-powered boreholes that the project did not rehabilitate are now running for fewer hours each day.

Staff turnover among hygiene promoters was high in Garissa, where other agencies offered longer contracts. The team responded by training volunteers from the water management committees as assistant promoters, which also strengthened the link between the committees and the sessions.

## 5. Gender and inclusion

Women and girls carry most of the burden of water collection in all programme areas, so the location of tap stands and the opening hours of water points were agreed with women's groups before the work started. Latrine designs include a handrail and a wider door for older people and people with disabilities, and households with a member who cannot walk to a water point were linked to neighbours who collect water for them through the committees.

Menstrual hygiene sessions were run by female promoters for girls and women only, and the kits include reusable pads chosen after consultation with schoolgirls in Turkana. Boys and men were reached through separate sessions at markets and livestock watering points, where promoters discussed the cost of illness in the family and the role of men in keeping water clean at home.

## 6. Monitoring and data quality

Field officers record activities on mobile forms that are synchronised whenever a connection is available. The monitoring officer checks the forms each week against the attendance sheets and the delivery notes before the figures are added to the indicator table. During the quarter a spot check in Marsabit found that some sessions had been recorded twice; the duplicates were removed before this report was written.

Water quality is tested at the point of collection and in a sample of households, because water that is safe at the tap can be contaminated during transport and storage. Results are shared with the county public health officers, who follow up any water point that fails a test.

## 7. Operations and logistics

Materials for latrines and borehole repairs were bought through framework agreements with suppliers in Nairobi and Garissa. Transport costs rose with the price of diesel, and the team reorganised deliveries so that each truck served several sites on one trip. The Lodwar warehouse was cleared and restocked in August, and a stock count at the end of the quarter matched the records.

The project worked with local fundis, who were trained by the county water department on borehole maintenance. Using local labour shortened the time needed for repairs and left skills in the communities that will be needed after the project ends.

## 8. Coordination

Northbridge Relief attended the county WASH coordination forums in Lodwar, Marsabit town and Garissa and reported its progress to the national WASH cluster each month (see the 4W matrix, sheet 3). Borehole rehabilitation sites were agreed with the county water departments to avoid duplication with two other agencies.

## 9. Lessons learned

Rehabilitating existing boreholes was faster and cheaper than drilling new ones, but only where the committees were ready to collect fees for maintenance. Where the committee was new, the team needed more time for training before handing over the water point. Next year the project will start committee training before the repair work begins, rather than after it.

Hygiene promotion had the most effect when it was linked to a visible change, such as a new tap stand or a handwashing station at the school. Sessions held without such a change drew fewer people and were less well remembered when the team asked participants about them a month later.

## 10. Next quarter

In the last quarter of the year the project will rehabilitate the remaining four boreholes and expand hygiene promotion to schools in Garissa. Cumulatively, the project has now reached nearly 17,000 people with hygiene promotion since April.
